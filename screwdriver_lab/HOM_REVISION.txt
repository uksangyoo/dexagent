HYDROELASTIC SCREWDRIVER REVISION

Scope
The accepted comparison is refined22: three adapters, three initial finger
clearances, six seconds per trial. All nine use identical source hashes,
contact parameters, actuator limits, stroke/load recipe and observation access.
Only the adapter and initial finger clearance change. The tool begins upright
above a prealigned fastener. No pickup or full-task success is credited.

Nominal results: fingertip paths 39.47 degrees / 0.116 mm; rigid object twist
26.10 degrees / 0.079 mm; relative contact motion 154.10 degrees / 0.434 mm.
Near/nominal/far clearances are 0.05/0.15/0.30 mm. Relative contact motion
produces 158.72/154.10/150.59 degrees. These rank these implementations under
this model, not all possible controllers in their abstraction families.

Human reference and paper
The upper-left human grasps horizontally, lifts, pivots the tool, supports the
swivel cap with the index and makes compact side-finger strokes with little
wrist motion. The visible reference presses on a table; screw insertion is
an extension of that demonstration. Hidden separation cannot be resolved from
one camera. The supplied paper is Relative Contact Velocity-Controlled
Hand-Object Mechanism for Dexterous Tool Manipulation (arXiv:2609.25619v2).
Its six relative coordinates motivate the adapter. This code does not reproduce
the paper's full geodesic observer or coupled-contact optimization. In
particular, the paper releases side contacts during reset; loaded sliding is
an additional requirement here.

Physics boundary
Drake 1.58 SAP integrates dynamics on CPU. MuJoCo supplies kinematics/Jacobians
and import audits only. No mj_step drives these records. The screwdriver and
screw are unactuated free rigid bodies. There is no applied tool/screw wrench,
attachment, helical equality, body gravity compensation or state correction
inside a task rollout. Initialization is restricted to t=0 in fresh rollouts. Explicit checkpoint
continuations restore the exact recorded position and velocity once at the
branch; they are separately labeled and never counted as uninterrupted runs. The separate
thread validation bench deliberately applies external force and torque and
is not scored as robot manipulation.

Twenty finger and six arm motors receive bounded efforts at 500 Hz. Finger
continuous limit is 0.11 Nm, with a conservative 0.65 Nm / 85 rpm envelope.
The common inner loop provides gravity compensation, tangential impedance,
normal-force feedback and anti-windup, all through those motor limits.

Fingertips use tetrahedral hydroelastic pressure fields with a 1.7 MPa
numerical pressure modulus (not a calibrated Young's modulus). Handle/cap
are rigid hydroelastic shapes, grip friction 0.6. Steel point fallback uses
explicit stiffness 1e8 N/m, friction 0.15 for threads, SAP relaxation 0.001 s,
and beta=0. The hydroelastic mesh setting is 2 mm, with 0.8 mm remeshed surface
edges. Physics dt=0.25 ms; helices use 48 convex sectors per revolution.
The bit/head is a simplified cruciform surrogate, not a measured Phillips
profile. Pressure-patch traction produces moments without an added soft-finger spin
term. This pressure approximation does not model calibrated tangential or
viscoelastic pad deformation. Dimensions, modulus, friction, rotor inertia
and damping remain declared assumptions, not measured hardware parameters.

The old wrist capsule extended beyond its flange and overlapped the thumb
base. It is replaced by the supplied wrist3 CAD convex envelope. Contacts
inside the same rigid assembly are excluded, including pad/backing and
neighboring thread sectors. The corrected initial audit has no fingertip/tool
overlap; the largest residual independent-body overlap is below 1 micrometre.
Old physical_task, slim and refined21 trials are historical diagnostics and
are not evidence of realistic full-task completion or comparative superiority.

Metrics and limitations
Useful loaded-sliding episodes require >=1.5 mm measured relative sliding,
load >0.01 N for >=95% of sliding samples, >=3 degrees forward screw motion,
and tilt <3 degrees. This is not proof of a complete finger reset. Phase names
and commanded strokes are not counted as physical success. Raw sample columns
are stored in each NPZ. Contact samples are at 500 Hz; states/commands at 50 Hz.
RTX videos replay every other recorded state at 25 fps, with no time scaling.
The purple witness is display-only and follows the actual handle transform.

An independent 96-sector thread bench changes rotation by 0.039% and depth by
0.57 micrometres when dt halves from 0.125 to 0.0625 ms. The chosen 48-sector,
0.25 ms bench differs about 3.6% from the finest case. A finer pad mesh changes
the two-second robot result from 43.36 to 40.08 degrees (7.6%). Halving the
robot physics step gives 151.77 rather than 154.10 degrees at six seconds
(1.51% difference), with seven useful loaded-sliding episodes in both. Lower grip
friction 0.4 loses the axis at 2.376 seconds. Numerical agreement in one bench
does not establish convergence or robustness of the complete task.

Files and reproduction
Extract full-pipeline.tar.xz for the licensed base robot geometry. Extract
hom-code-evidence.zip into that same root. hom-recordings.zip contains all
nine immutable refined22 JSON/XML/NPZ records. The code archive includes the
current implementation, immutable source snapshots indexed by SHA256, the
initialization fixture/seed, selected geometry caches, validation evidence,
and SHA256SUMS.json. Compare each report's source_sha256 and xml_sha256 with
the archived originals. Portability changes and later experiments do not
rewrite these records.

Recorded environment: Python 3.12, Drake 1.58.0, MuJoCo 3.10.1.dev950629900,
NumPy 2.5.1, SciPy 1.18.0, trimesh 5.0.0rc1, tetgen 0.8.4,
pymeshlab 2025.7.post1. These include development versions. See
hom-environment.txt for the installed package list. Rendering used Blender
Cycles 4.5 / OptiX. Only physical GPU 1 (PCI 61:00) is permitted for GPU work.
This backend runs physics on CPU; GPU 1 is used for RTX rendering.

From the extracted root, with that environment activated:
  CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m unittest screwdriver_lab.hom.test_contact_graph
  CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m screwdriver_lab.hom.audit_scene
  CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m screwdriver_lab.hom.experiment --name reproduce-hom --interface hom --duration 6 --speed 60 --dt .00025 --thread-segments 48 --explicit-steel --beta 0 --cycle alternating --drive-load .4 --return-load .06 --hybrid --tilt 0
Repeat with --interface cartesian or object, and --gap .00005 or .00030.
The current source explicitly removes passive cap armature (Drake already
ignored that unsupported value) and adds failure/seating guards. For the
exact refined22 source, use hom.reproduce with a separate destination:
  python -m screwdriver_lab.hom.reproduce --destination /tmp/hom-refined22-reproduction
Then run the printed command from that directory. Asset paths in the input
fixture are relocated, so textual XML hashes will differ outside the original
workspace. Dynamics code snapshots remain byte-for-byte unchanged. A new
run is never written over an existing accepted record.

The graph is executable local code with observation-conditioned transitions,
inspired by Graph-as-Policy's structure. It is not its harness/schema and does
not run an LLM during these trials. Contact/state observations are privileged.

Seating-stop development (separate from frozen comparison)
The prepared initial configuration leaves 1.943659 mm of flat-seat clearance,
not 2 mm: the seed already places the screw about 56 micrometres lower than
the nominal construction. hom-seating23 physically reaches the seat but its
old 1.95 mm stop guard never fires. It continues turning and loses alignment
at 32.098 s. That original failed run is immutable.

hom-seat-stop40/41/42 branch from its first saved state satisfying the new
seat-contact/clearance guard, at 28.022 s. Position and velocity continuity
errors are 1.11e-16. Only controller references are reset; all subsequent
motion uses bounded motor effort with unchanged contact physics. Stop40 and
41 fail the continuous-force hold criterion. Stop42 lowers side loads to
0.2 N and raises index load to 1.2 N; it passes 0.5 s continuously with seat
load >0.2 N, head maximum clearance <20 micrometres, screw speed <5 deg/s,
tilt <3 deg, side loads >0.05 N and index load >0.1 N. It ends at 29.090 s,
695.825 degrees / 1.940596 mm. Final head clearance spans -0.008 to 6.133
micrometres. Minimum seat force over the accepted interval is 0.20566 N;
maximum screw speed is 0.01222 deg/s. This is a checkpoint stop validation,
not a fresh uninterrupted seating success and not a tightening-torque test.
The corrected fresh-run guard and 1.2 N cap hold are implemented, but have
not yet completed a new full-length run. hom-seat-stop42-observations.npz
contains the exact original drive prefix plus the continued states/samples.

Pickup remains incomplete. Development trial36 grasps, lifts and pivots
about 74 degrees while the two side contacts remain loaded, but fails to
establish stable cap support and drops the tool. Its video includes failure.
Later trials and their changed controller sources are retained locally;
none is included in the accepted abstraction ranking.

All three nominal controller interfaces were also tested at half timestep:
Cartesian 34.56 versus 39.47 deg; rigid object twist 18.08 versus 26.10 deg;
relative contact 151.77 versus 154.10 deg. The ordering persists, but the
baselines are materially more timestep-sensitive. Friction 0.8 reduces HOM
progress to 88.70 deg; friction 0.4 loses the axis. These expose limits to
robustness rather than provide evidence of hardware-calibrated friction.
