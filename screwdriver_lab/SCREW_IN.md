# Contact-driven screw insertion

The task is to drive a pre-threaded, pre-aligned screw **1 mm into a workpiece** with a Tesollo DG5F-S hand. This corrects the earlier free-rotation proxy, which did not represent fastening.

The human reference supplies a qualitative sequence: cap support, side-finger turns and repeated regrasp. Robot roles are retargeted to the hand geometry: thumb on the passive swivel cap, index and middle fingers on the handle. The remaining fingers are parked. The hand has 20 actuated joints and an axial-only carriage with force feedback; no wrist rotation is available. Driver and screw are unactuated.

## Mechanical model

MuJoCo 3.10.1 integrates the model at 1 ms. The 24 mm diameter handle, swivel cap, 6 mm flat bit, slotted head, 1 mm pitch and 1 mm insertion distance are explicit benchmark choices, not calibrated measurements from the video. The driver is a free body; its physical bit contacts the screw slot. The screw's unactuated hinge and slide are coupled by a joint equality: angle = 2π × depth / pitch. This is an ideal guided helical mechanism with 0.0008 N·m hinge friction, not meshed thread physics. Visual thread rings do not collide.

The screw head physically seats on a workpiece. The benchmark excludes screw pickup, slot search, cutting threads, cross-threading, uncertain sensing, full arm kinematics and hardware calibration. Finger meshes use MuJoCo's convex collision approximation. The hand and carriage have ideal gravity compensation.

Success requires all of these continuously for 0.4 s:

- Depth ≥0.98 mm; bit lateral error <1.2 mm and tilt <8°; bit/slot contact >0.05 N.
- Screw speed <0.15 rad/s; downward cap force between 0.3 and 5 N.
- Screw-head/workpiece seating contact >0.1 N.

Commands run at 50 Hz with ±0.3 N·m finger limits. The shared guard checks every physics step and stops on >8 N finger load, >0.5 mm contact penetration, shaft contact >0.2 N, alignment loss or engagement loss. A guard is a detection/termination mechanism, not a guarantee against one-step numerical/contact spikes.

## Implementations and results

All controllers share the reset, 1.5 s pressure acquisition, 35 s total budget, axial force feedback, actuator bounds and physical guards. Joint and synergy baselines use a nominal single-turn calibration generated by the geometric controller. These are complete scripted systems, not a controlled test isolating abstraction level or LLM intelligence.

| Contract | Implementation | Nominal depth | 20° slot depth | Low-friction depth |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Joint targets | Replay a calibrated 20-joint single turn | 0.088 mm | 0.089 mm | 0.087 mm |
| Synergies | Three PCA coordinates from that motor bank | 0.088 mm | 0.089 mm | 0.087 mm |
| Fingertips | Damped Jacobian IK, cap support and bounded 30° side motion | 0.103 mm | 0.098 mm | 0.102 mm |
| Screw motion | Actual screw-angle feedback, bounded 40° local roll | 0.125 mm | 0.128 mm | 0.124 mm |
| Contact skill | Press, turn, release, return, regrasp, seat | **1.001 mm, seated** | **1.001 mm, seated** | **0.816 mm, invalid contact failure** |

The first four have a single-turn range and stop after 2 s without meaningful insertion progress. The contact controller adds a repeated gait and succeeds in two of three conditions, at 29.02 and 27.58 s. This does not show that raw joint or Cartesian interfaces cannot complete the task; they need a long-horizon sequence too. No live LLM, vision learner or training procedure is part of these rollouts.

The low-friction contact trial terminates at 19.987 s when one contact sample jumps from small penetration to **3.641 mm** and **56.217 N** peak finger force. The entire failed recording, including this triggering state, is retained. It is not evidence of a physically valid full insertion. Both successful runs remain below 2.49 N finger load and 0.051 mm penetration, including reset. Sliding contact friction is 0.9 in nominal/slot cases and 0.25 in the low-friction case, applied throughout the scene, including bit/slot contact. Thread resistance is held fixed. The slot and bit are rotated together by 20° at reset for the orientation case.

## Reproduce

Use Python 3.12 with `requirements.txt`, the included Tesollo model, and FFmpeg. Run commands from the repository/bundle root:

```bash
# Optional: refit the geometric reset. The frozen fit is included.
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m screwdriver_lab.screw_scene

# Generate the nominal single-turn calibration used by joints/synergy.
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m screwdriver_lab.screw_control

# Five methods × three paired conditions; raw states and traces.
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m screwdriver_lab.run_screw

# Mechanical and evidence checks, including a 35 s pressure-only control.
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m screwdriver_lab.validate_screw

# RTX: adjust the Blender Python path in run_screw.py for your installation.
# This workstation uses physical GPU 1 only. GPU 0 belongs to another task.
python -m dextrain.resource_guard --gpu 1 \
  --output screwdriver_lab/artifacts/screw_in/new-render-guard --seconds 7200 \
  -- python -m screwdriver_lab.run_screw --render
```

Physics interpreter on this host: `/home/uyoo/miniconda3/envs/newton/bin/python`.
Renderer: `/home/uyoo/miniconda3/envs/blender-render/bin/python`, `bpy==4.5.10`, NumPy, Pillow. `render_screw.py` restricts CUDA visibility to GPU 1, requires OptiX and refuses CPU fallback. It replays exact exported MuJoCo geometry and poses at 1280×960, 30 fps, 24 samples with denoising. The inset camera uses the same state. Rendering changes materials/lights/cameras, not physics. Each clip includes acquisition and a labeled one-second final freeze. Render manifests record device PCI ID, source hash and timing.

Artifacts are under `screwdriver_lab/artifacts/screw_in/`: `*.xml` scenes, `*.npz` full qpos, `*.json` measured state/trace, `manifest.json` paired experiment data, `validation.json` check results, `precision_grasp.json` and `motor_bank.npz` frozen initialization/calibration. The website's `data/screw-in.json` adds video paths and render provenance. Mesh paths in archived XML are rewritten by the exporter; new simulations build paths from the bundle location.

The source ZIP contains code, meshes and recorded states. Media packs contain the new videos and human crop; extract all into the same directory for offline playback. Use `python -m screwdriver_lab.serve_screw` for a loopback preview with byte-range support for video scrubbing. The hosted site's historical rotation archive is not part of the standalone screw-in source bundle.
